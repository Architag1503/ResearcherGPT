Abstract—Modern scientific inquiry is constrained by the rapid
expansion of academic literature, rendering exhaustive synthe-
sis, contradiction discovery, and layout-preserving manuscript
compilation difficult to perform manually. Existing Retrieval-
Augmented Generation (RAG) paradigms can suffer from seman-
tic fragmentation, context dilution across multi-hop relationships,
hallucinated bibliographies, and loss of non-textual artifacts such
as LATEX equations, tables, and multimodal figures. This paper
presents ResearcherGPT, a distributed cognitive framework for
automating the academic literature pipeline. Its four principal
components are: (1) a tri-hybrid retrieval engine combining dense
embeddings (d= 3072ord= 384), sparse BM25 lexical retrieval,
and Neo4j property-graph traversal through Reciprocal Rank
Fusion (RRF), with a graph boost factor ofβ= 1.30; (2)
a LangGraph multi-agent state machine backed by Redis and
coordinating thirteen specialized agents with fallback routing
across Groq, Gemini, SambaNova, and OpenAI; (3) FormaTeX,
a layout-aware PDF extraction and manuscript synthesis engine
targeting IEEE, ACM, Springer, and Elsevier styles; and (4) an
eleven-phase integrity pipeline combiningk-shingle MinHash,
Winnowing fingerprints, Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) edit dis-
tance, and external OpenAlex bibliographic resolution. The sup-
plied evaluation reports 96.4% citation factual validity, a 41.8%
reduction in cross-agent synchronization latency, and 92.7% RRF
Recall@5. These figures are reproduced as supplied and are not
independently validated in this typesetting conversion.
Index Terms—Multi-agent systems, retrieval-augmented gener-
ation, knowledge graphs, reciprocal rank fusion, scientific paper

I. INTRODUCTION
The velocity of peer-reviewed publication has surpassed hu-
man comprehension capacity. Across computational sciences,
large numbers of preprints and journal articles are indexed
annually across repositories and scholarly databases such
as arXiv, IEEE Xplore, and PubMed. Scholars must digest
large volumes of literature, isolate methodological contribu-
tions, detect empirical contradictions between experiments,
identify open research gaps, and draft publication-compliant
manuscripts.
Large Language Models (LLMs), including GPT-family
models, Gemini, and LLaMA-family models, demonstrate
strong linguistic capabilities. However, their naive application
to scholarly synthesis exposes several limitations:
•Context fragmentation and semantic drift:conventional
RAG systems partition documents into token chunks.
Dense vector retrieval may fail to preserve relations such
as a method being evaluated on a particular dataset and
compared against a baseline.
•Bibliographic hallucination:generative models may pro-
duce nonexistent Digital Object Identifiers (DOIs), com-
bine real author names with fabricated titles, or attribute
claims to sources that do not support them.
•Loss of scientific artifacts:academic documents contain
mathematical notation, multi-column tables, figures, and
diagrams. Text-only PDF extraction can flatten these
structures and scramble equations or table relationships.
•Agent deadlocks and state corruption:multi-agent review
and synthesis workflows can encounter cyclic dependen-
cies, concurrent state-update races, and excessive context
growth when state history is not partitioned and check-
pointed.
We introduce ResearcherGPT, a distributed academic
workspace and multi-agent framework designed to automate
literature synthesis while grounding generated claims in
retrieved evidence.
A. Problem Statement and Scope
An autonomous research assistant for this setting should
satisfy five operational criteria:
1)Relational grounding:combine semantic intent, lexi-
cal precision, and ontological relationships, such as a
method evaluated on a dataset and compared with a
baseline.
2)Attribution veracity:associate generated claims with
primary evidence chunks and sentence-level provenance.
3)Structural integrity:preserve mathematical syntax, tab-
ular data, and visual schematics through extraction,
reasoning, and recompilation.

B. System Overview
ResearcherGPT uses a decoupled three-tier architecture. The
client tier is based on Next.js 15 and React 19, with Zustand
state synchronization, a TipTap drafting canvas, KaTeX for-
mula rendering, and a WebGL-based conceptual visualization.
The API tier uses Node.js and Express with TypeScript,
BullMQ, and Redis for asynchronous job scheduling, and
MongoDB Atlas for document persistence. The cognitive tier
is a Python FastAPI service hosting LangGraph workflows,
Qdrant vector search, Neo4j knowledge graphs, document
extraction, and FormaTeX compilation.
The principal contributions are:
1) A tri-hybrid rank-fusion mechanism combining dense
embeddings, BM25 lexical retrieval, and Neo4j graph
traversal with a graph-context boost.
2) A LangGraph workflow coordinating thirteen special-
ized agents through shared typed state and a model-
routing fallback registry.
3) An eleven-phase integrity pipeline combining lexical,
semantic, structural, formula, figure, table, code, and
bibliographic checks.
4) FormaTeX, a layout-aware extraction and compilation
engine that reconstructs tables and mathematical struc-
tures and emits manuscripts in multiple publication
styles.
II. LITERATUREREVIEW ANDRELATEDWORK
A. Evolution of Retrieval-Augmented Generation
Retrieval-Augmented Generation was formalized by Lewis
et al. to mitigate parametric-memory hallucinations by ground-
ing model outputs in retrieved documents [1]. Early implemen-
tations used dense vector representations from dual-encoder
architectures. Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) demonstrated
the effectiveness of learned query–passage representations
for open-domain question answering [2], while Sentence-
BERT enabled semantically meaningful sentence embeddings
through Siamese network training [3]. Hierarchical Navigable
Small World (HNSW) graphs provide efficient approximate
nearest-neighbor search [4]. However, dense retrieval can miss
exact technical identifiers, uncommon terminology, and ex-
plicit lexical matches. BM25 remains a strong sparse retrieval
baseline [5]. Reciprocal Rank Fusion combines heterogeneous
ranked lists without requiring score calibration [6].
B. Knowledge Graphs and Multi-Hop Retrieval
Graph-based retrieval augments vector similarity with ex-
plicit entity and relation structure. GraphRAG uses graph
communities and summaries to support query-focused syn-
thesis across distributed information [7]. ResearcherGPT uses
a project-scoped property graph to represent papers, authors,
methods, datasets, and concepts. This representation is in-
tended to support retrieval paths that are difficult to recover
from isolated text chunks.
C. Multi-Agent Systems in Scientific Discovery
Frameworks such as AutoGen, CrewAI, and LangGraph
support decomposition of complex language-model workflows
into role-specific agents and graph-based execution [8]–[10].
Scholarly tools such as Semantic Scholar, Elicit, and Con-
sensus support literature discovery; ResearcherGPT focuses
on iterative synthesis, claim cross-examination, contradiction
discovery, and manuscript compilation.
D. Layout-Aware Document Extraction
Academic PDF extraction is difficult because visual-spatial
information is encoded through positioned text, drawing prim-
itives, and bounding boxes. PyMuPDF and pdfplumber expose
layout information useful for reconstructing reading order
and tables [11]. ResearcherGPT combines coordinate-based
heuristics with vision-assisted fallbacks to handle non-standard
figures, inline matrices, and scanned pages.
E. Plagiarism Detection and Academic Integrity
Winnowing uses local fingerprints to detect shared sub-
strings while controlling fingerprint density [12]. MinHash es-
timates Jaccard similarity between shingle sets [13]. Academic
integrity assessment also requires semantic and structural
checks, as discussed in the literature on plagiarism detection
[14]. ResearcherGPT combines these approaches with formula
AST comparison and bibliographic resolution.

G. Limitations and Ethical Considerations
The principal design trade-off in ResearcherGPT is between
fluent generative synthesis and strict factual grounding. A
confidence threshold ofθ= 0.85in the Fact Verification Agent
is described as reducing citation errors to 0.2%; however, strict
verification can constrain the Writing Agent from proposing
novel theoretical connections. A dual-track drafting mode
could distinguish verified historical claims from exploratory
hypotheses.
The enterprise stack comprising Neo4j, Qdrant, Redis,
MongoDB, and multiple model-provider endpoints introduces
operational complexity and API cost. For libraries exceed-
ing 1,000 papers, local graph synchronization latency may
exceed 120 seconds. BullMQ background workers decouple
ingestion from user-facing query interfaces, although further
optimization remains necessary. ResearcherGPT is intended
to support literature aggregation, contradiction discovery, and
formatting compliance; it is not intended to replace original
human scholarship or bypass peer review. Its integrity engine
audits drafts for verbatim copying, paraphrased borrowing, and
synthetic-text distribution markers.
H. Extended Technical Roadmap
The following extensions are proposed as future research
directions rather than claims of completed implementation:
1)Reinforcement-learned adaptive agent routing:the cur-
rent deterministic DAG assigns a predefined sequence of
agents, with conditional peer-review branches. A future
adaptive policy could select agent actions from the cur-
rent state snapshot using a Partially Observable Markov
Decision Process (POMDP), with Proximal Policy Op-
timization (PPO) or Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)
as candidate strategies. The policy would dynamically
allocate reasoning and verification effort while retaining
fact-checking gates.
2)Formal proof checking and reproducibility:Lean 4 and
Mathlib could provide a proof-checking interface for
mathematical claims that admit formalization. A sand-
box based on WebAssembly or eBPF could execute
selected code artifacts under isolation, supporting repro-
ducibility checks for extracted algorithms and empirical
metrics. These mechanisms would complement, not re-
place, source-level verification.
3)Vector graphics and collaborative research:program-
matic coordinate compilation through TikZ and Asymp-
tote could preserve scalable visual artifacts during
manuscript generation. A CRDT-based collaborative
canvas (e.g., Yjs with TipTap and WebSockets) could
support concurrent human–agent editing and conflict-
free synchronization. Continuous ingestion through
graph and scholarly APIs could further keep project
knowledge meshes current.
VI. CONCLUSION ANDFUTUREWORK
A. Comprehensive Synthesis and Retrospective Conclusion
ResearcherGPT is presented as a distributed multi-agent
framework for scholarly literature synthesis, layout preserva-
tion, and attribution verification. Conventional RAG systems
remain limited by semantic context dilution, bibliographic
and metric hallucinations, loss of visual-spatial structures,
and brittle orchestration under shared-state contention or API
failures.
ResearcherGPT addresses these challenges through four in-
tegrated capabilities. Tri-Hybrid Reciprocal Rank Fusion com-

and graph traversal (Neo4j). In the reported evaluation, this de-
sign achieved MRR@10 of 0.879 and Recall@5 of 0.927, link-
ing method- and dataset-related chunks to queries. A thirteen-
agent LangGraph workflow coordinates extraction, synthesis,
contradiction analysis, verification, and review through typed
state and transactional synchronization. FormaTeX recon-

The current design includes retrieval fusion, agent orches-

RL-guided adaptive graph routing using MCTS/PPO; (ii) Lean

(iv) a streaming federated academic knowledge mesh; (v) We-
